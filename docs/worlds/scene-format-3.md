This is the language of a scene — a place of a living, animated world, with its mood: what you write when you paint a scene or change one (as the person you work for describes the world or a place in it, or as a game played on the world needs). A scene is the files of its folder, `scenes/<folder>/`: `scene.json` — the spec below without its code — and its code, each piece in a file of its own (`worlds/README.md`: The repository); where the schema below has code in a field, that field is its file. Everything in the scene — sky, sun, moon, stars, clouds, land, water, trees, buildings, people, weather, glints — you paint yourself, with code, for this scene.

A scene runs on moood's engine 3 (WebGPU, on the GPU): it says "engine": 3. What you paint is painted once — a picture of how a thing is now, in its state; everything that moves, sways, drifts, flickers or shimmers is moved by the engine on the GPU, as you tell it (layers, animate, shaders, particles); what happens in the scene happens by events — actions that take as long as they take, sequences that wait for each other and for the game — not by tables of seconds. The engine is a medium with its own grain: what it does well it does at any size and smoothly; what it does not do is not in this text — and is not to be reached another way (The engine's limits, below).

  "caption": string,               // one quiet line shown over the scene, in English

  "modules": [string],             // the world's modules this scene uses, by name (optional, below)

color = "#rrggbb".
A word the engine does not know — in the spec, in an element, a layer's options, a light, particles — is said once in the scene's warnings, naming where, and not taken: write only the words of this text.

Build the world, not the frame. A scene is laid out for a frame of 4:3 to 21:9 — the safe frame — and its height is the measure. The scene is laid out on a 16:9 stage as tall as the frame: a 16:9 screen shows all of it, a 4:3 one only its middle (x 0.125..0.875), a 21:9 one also 0.16 beyond either side. Keep what matters within x 0.125..0.875; let everything else run on past the edges. The safe frame is only where the story and the controls are: every screen shows it whole, and whatever a screen shows round it — one wider than 21:9 far to the sides, a phone held upright far above and below — is the world going on, drawn as it is (no bars): lay the ground, the land, the far things and the sky wide enough that the world does not end there. scene.view and e.view tell what the screen shows now — { safe, seen }, each [left, top, right, bottom] in stage heights (x 0..16/9 the stage, y 0..1 its height): the safe frame, and all the screen; they change as the window does and are not state (nothing in the state may depend on the screen). The world is truly 3D, in metres: the viewer stands at the start with eyes on the "horizon" line and can walk forward/back, step sideways and look up/down; near things move more than far ones, what stands nearer hides what stands behind it, pixel by pixel. Far things are hazed toward hazeColor; walking closer clears it.
"camera" — how the viewer moves through the scene (default walk):
- "picture" — a still picture: the view breathes and leans a little toward the pointer or the phone's tilt, near things shifting against far ones: one composed picture (a box, a stage, a room seen from its open side).
- "walk" — at eye height, within "area" [left, right, back, front] metres of the start, else as far as the things standing in it. The start is as far back as the viewer can be and there they can't step aside — the foreground you paint holds the frame; coming closer they may. The wheel walks toward what is truly painted under the pointer — stopping where the thing about fills the frame; a wall or a board that already fills it is walked up to — and back no farther than the start; a drag steps aside and lifts the gaze. "follow": elements the camera keeps in view as the viewer walks (characters who walk and run); a film's camera_target (below) frames instead.
- "panorama" — a long place passed along sideways (an alley, a street, a shore, a train window): as far as "span" [left, right] metres (else as far as the things standing within 40 m — not the sky); lay it out with "across". The span holds where the viewer takes the view — his own way along added to camera_x; camera_x itself is the scene's word, never held: let the span cover every camera_x the scene sets (one outside it is said in the warnings, and from there the viewer may only come back toward the span). The scene brings the view to a place by moving camera_x — scene.move, over seconds (not scene.path) —, and the viewer's own way aside goes back along that move, ending where it brings the view; while a finger holds the view, what it holds stays under it — so camera_x does not tell what is seen: never work out from it which place the viewer looks at. Set at once, camera_x leaves the viewer's way aside as it is, as far as the span lets it (the view jumps with camera_x). The wheel walks in toward what is under the pointer as in "walk", and back to the start, no farther.
- "overhead" — raised "height" metres (8), looking down "look" screen heights (0.25): a table, a board game, a town from a rooftop; what stands stays upright like figures on a board.
- "around" — one spot, the head turned all round (or "turn" degrees either way) and up and down: a clearing by a fire, a tower's top. "roam" — walking about a place, looking anywhere, as in a room ("area", else among the things around). Place elements by "bearing" (degrees from straight ahead, right +, 180 behind) and "distance"; the sky is the full circle: x 0..e.aspect all round, straight ahead at e.aspect/2 — paint it so the ends meet (sin, cos of x with whole periods). The viewer's own floor is a plane with "distance" 0.01.
- "platform" — below.
"on": an element's id — the camera rides it (a ship, a carriage, a balloon's basket: the anchor its deck and its people stand "on", moved through the world by its "place"), as people are carried: it starts at that element's anchor, looking the way "distance" grows on it, and goes and turns with it wherever in the world that is. The viewer's own moves and "area", the operator (where it may stand, the shots it finds), camera_x, camera_z, camera_yaw are all in that element's frame; the eye is as high over its face under it (a deck heeling carries the eye, the horizon stays level — camera_roll tips it). Lay what it carries round its anchor as a scene is laid round the start; the world it passes lies where it is. In any camera but a platformer's.

An element:
  { "id": string,                       // short stable id ("pier", "moon"); when editing a scene keep each element's id, new elements get new ones
    "name": string (in English),
    "sky": true,                        // an element of the sky (sky, sun, moon, stars, clouds): infinitely far, never moves when walking
    "x": 0..1,                          // otherwise: where its anchor is, across the stage (seen from the start)
    "across": metres,                   // ... or instead: metres to the right of the start's line of sight (left negative) — for a place longer than the view
    "bearing": degrees,                 // ... or, looking around: which way from the start (with "distance")
    "facing": degrees,                  // turned its own way (a wall, a door, a house's side), in any camera, about its anchor: where its face looks (180 toward the start; 90 to the right — its +x then runs away from the start)
    "tilt": degrees,                    // leaned back from upright about its anchor's line: a lid thrown open, a board leaning (−90…90; what slopes for good is a plane)
    "roll": degrees,                    // turned in its own face about its anchor, its right edge rising: a wheel, a clock's hand, a thing built of cards on an unseen anchor that pitches as seen from the side — an aeroplane climbing (rolled first, then tilted, then turned by "facing")
    "y": 0..1  OR  "distance": metres,  // where it touches the ground on screen, or how far ahead it is (behind the start: below 0 — with "across", not "x" or "bearing")
    "elevation": metres,                // anchor height above the ground, default 0
    "height": metres,                   // its real height: the box and the code are in metres at the size it gives
    "box": [left, top, right, bottom],  // metres around the anchor that the painting fits in; default [-0.8h, -1.1h, 0.8h, 0.15h] — paint past it is cut off straight (the engine says so); a sky element's: stage heights, what it paints (below)
    "color": color,                     // default fill/stroke
    "code": string,                     // JS function body painting it — once per state (below)
    "animate": string,                  // JS function body run every frame: what moves (below)
    "shader": string, "uniforms": { name: number }, // its own WGSL: how its painting looks each moment on the GPU (below)
    "flicker": 0..1,                    // how much the glows it paints (e.light) flicker
    "glass": true | { "strength": 0..1 (0.25) }, // a pane of glass: the room mirrored in it (below)
    "place": { "across"|"distance"|"elevation"|"x"|"bearing"|"facing"|"tilt"|"roll"|"rotation": state key, ... }, // it moves (or turns) through the world: where the state says (below)
    "on": string,                       // it stands on that element and goes and turns with it; its place and facing are in that one's own turn (below)
    "copies": [ { "across", "distance", ... }, ... ], // the same painting standing in many places (below)
    "behind": [string], "inFront": [string], // drawn behind / in front of these, whatever the depths (below)
    "clip": { "inside" | "outside": [string] }, // seen only within / out of what these paint (cards and planes; below)
    "unseen": true,                     // not shown: an anchor others stand on, a point the camera looks at (no code needed), a shape others are clipped by
    "seen": "reflection",               // seen only in mirrors (planes that "reflect", panes of "glass") (below)
    "insert": state key, "iris": [x, y, rx, ry], // a shot of its own over the scene (below)
    "shadow": true | { "light": id, "onto": ["ground" | element id, ...], "strength": 0..1 }, // (below)
    "glows": true,                      // the scene's light doesn't darken it: a lamp, a fire, a lit window
    "inside": true,                     // it is in a room: lit by the light that comes in (light "inside", below)
    "translucent": 0..1,                // something thin — a sail, leaves, paper, a curtain: how much of the light behind it comes through (below)
    "onLand": false,                    // a picture of the land itself, not set on the relief (below)
    "redraw": [state key, ...] | { key: step }, // only these keys repaint it, once moved by `step` (a big plane on a key moving smoothly), whatever else it reads
    "script": string }                  // this element's own mechanics, JS (below)

Painting — "code", the body of function (g, e): g is a CanvasRenderingContext2D. For a normal element it is in METRES, origin at the anchor, x to the right, y DOWN — so up is negative (a 6 m house spans y −6…0). For a sky element it is in SCREEN HEIGHTS from the stage's top-left corner: x 0..e.aspect is the stage, y 0..1 (by default paint x from −0.3 to e.aspect + 0.3 — wide screens see past the stage — and y from −0.35 to 1.25 — the viewer can look up and down; a sky element's "box" says how far it paints: [left, top, right, bottom] in its stage heights, up to a whole turn across and from −3 to 4 down; past what it paints the sky carries on by itself, below). Fill and stroke with paths (Path2D and addPath too), arcs, bezier curves, gradients, g.globalAlpha, composite modes (globalCompositeOperation: 'destination-out', 'lighter'), 'evenodd' fills for holes, transforms (getTransform, setTransform, DOMMatrix), text (font, fillText, measureText — a text a person reads: tr('…'), in English), shadows and filters (filter: blur() — their lengths are your units, like everything else). A picture the code builds in a canvas of its own it may draw (drawImage); the canvas it paints into it cannot read back. No DOM, no network, no timers — just drawing.
  A painting is STATIC: it paints what the thing is now, in its state — never in time. It is recorded once and the engine shows it at any size, sharp however close the viewer comes, painting only the parts that are seen; it is painted again only when a state key it read changed (a clock's `hour` repaints the clock's face alone). It never reads time: no e.t, no clock — motion is the engine's (layers, animate, shaders, particles: below), and a painting that repainted every frame would cost every frame.
  Paint code sees only its easel. It runs apart from the scene — apart from animate and from the scripts — and is given the state, `e`, the shared code and nothing else: it keeps nothing between paintings and shares nothing with animate or scripts but the state's keys and what the painting itself declares (its points, lights, layers, parts). The lib and the world's modules are run once for the paintings and once for the scene: a value one side leaves in them the other never sees — what both must know is a state key. It paints the same for the same state, whatever was painted before. e gives:
  - e.state — the scene's values (below): each key it reads repaints it when that key changes; e.id; e.rnd() — seeded random 0..1, the same every painting (seeded by the element's id); e.aspect — the stage's width in its heights (the sky's full circle looking around: 2π); e.area — its box ([left, top, right, bottom], its units); e.spot — where it stands ({ across, distance, elevation, ground }, metres); e.ground(across, distance) — the land's height there;
  - e.light(x, y, radius, color, strength, name?) — a glow the engine draws (a soft sprite, blooming in the night, flickering by "flicker") and a source of light reaching twice its radius (its id the element's, or "id#name");
  - e.at(x, y, name?) — a point of it as painted: with no name, where it is (its head): its lines and widgets follow it; with a name, one the game and the camera may aim at ("farm#window") — e.at, e.part, e.layer, e.spot, e.ground under Space;
  - e.form(fn, opts) — it is not flat: a form (below);
  - e.debug(value, x?, y?) — a note shown only with ?debug (a module's poses), at the point if given; e.warn(text) — wrong but not an error, said once in the scene's warnings;
  - e.anim, e.kit.<module>, e.lib — shared code (below).
  Light is the engine's: a figure painted flat in its daylight colours is lit by the scene — rims from the lamp beside it, its back dark against a window; don't paint light that moves or depends on where the lights are. Paint in daylight colours and let the scene's light make the evening.
  What you paint opaque costs the frame least: opaque paint hides what is behind it and is drawn once; paint that is see-through — mist, smoke, soft edges, a sky's layers — is laid over all that is behind it, pixel by pixel. Paint solid things solid, to their edges.

Motion — what moves without being painted again:
  Layers: a painting may be several layers, each painted once, each moving on its own: e.layer(name, opts, g => { … }) inside the code paints one (in the element's units, over its box unless opts.box says another). opts:
  - "pivot": [x, y] — what it turns and scales about (a door's hinge, a lantern's hook, a clock's axle, the root of a tail);
  - "wind": strength 0..1, or { "strength", "gust" 0..1 (how much it comes in gusts), "scale" (how big the gusts are) } — it bends about its pivot, more toward its free end, as the scene's light.wind blows; neighbours sway together, never in step (grass, a willow's crown, a flag, reeds, washing on a line);
  - "drift": [x, y] — it scrolls, its units a second, wrapping round its box (clouds, mist, a sheet of smoke, a river's flow): paint it so its ends meet;
  - "flicker": 0..1 — its brightness trembles (a fire's light on a wall, a neon sign);
  - "shimmer": 0..1 — it wavers (heat over a fire, a picture under water);
  - "blend": "normal" | "add" (light: a glow, a flame) | "multiply" (a shadow, a stain);
  - "depth": metres it stands before its card (+) or behind it (−) — a branch before the trunk, a curtain behind the window frame;
  - "box": [left, top, right, bottom] — its own box, when not the element's.
  Animate — "animate", the body of function (e), runs every frame, cheaply, and paints nothing: it moves the layers and the points, sets the shader's uniforms, dims the glows. e gives: e.t (seconds), e.dt, e.cut (seconds since the last cut: what has its own inertia starts afresh after it); e.id, e.aspect (as its painting's: a sky's layer carried round it); e.view (what the screen shows: above); e.state (the values now), e.was(key, secondsAgo), e.speed(key) (per second) — what lags behind reads them: a coat's tail, hair, a head following the body; e.layer(name) — its .rotate (radians, about its pivot, clockwise), .x, .y (offset, its units), .scale (.scaleX, .scaleY), .alpha, .brightness; e.uniform(name, value) (what it gives the GPU — a uniform, a layer's motion, a glow — must be a number: NaN or Infinity is not taken, the last kept, and said); e.glow(name, k) — its glow ("name"; null the unnamed one) at k of its strength now (a candle guttering, a lamp switched); e.at(x, y, name?) — a point that moves, marked this frame (a swinging lantern's flame, a head the camera follows); e.point(ref) — where a point of the world is in its painting now ([x, y]; a person's "david#hand_r", an element's "lamp#wick", or [across, distance, elevation] metres, the elevation over the land there, or {"on": id, "at": […]} in an element's frame); e.where(ref) — the same point in the world ({ across, distance, elevation }); e.spot — where it stands; e.mem — an object of its own kept from frame to frame (a spring, a phase added up by dt, a swing): what it remembers lives there, saved and restored with the scene; e.hero — a platformer's hero as it runs ({ vx, vy, ground }); e.burst(count) — particles' (below); e.anim, e.kit, e.lib; e.debug, e.warn. The arrays and objects it is given are reused: read them, don't keep them. Setting a value it already has costs nothing — a scene that stands still is not drawn again for it. An animate does not run while its element is not there to be seen at all — a closed insert, a person who is not here.
  For example, a lantern swinging on its hook, pushed by the wind:
    code: e.layer('lantern', { pivot: [0, -2.4] }, g => { …the chain and the lantern hanging from (0, −2.4)…; e.light(0, -1.5, 0.4, '#ffcc88', 0.8, 'flame'); });
    animate: const m = e.mem; m.a ??= 0; m.v ??= 0; m.v += (-9 * m.a - 0.6 * m.v + 0.4 * Math.sin(e.t * 0.7)) * e.dt; m.a += m.v * e.dt; e.layer('lantern').rotate = m.a;
  — the lantern painted once; it swings on the GPU, its flame's light with it.
  Shaders — "shader": WGSL of its own, for what a painting and layers can't do — fire, water's glints, a starry sky's twinkle, lightning, a screen's flicker. The text is one function and, above it, whatever it needs (functions, consts):
    fn shade(painted: vec4f, at: vec2f, uv: vec2f) -> vec4f
  — given its painting here (`painted`: premultiplied colour, linear), `at` the point of its painting (its units, y down), `uv` 0..1 over its box — returning the colour there (premultiplied). Given by the engine, by these names:
    layer(k, at) -> vec4f — its painting (k 0) or its layers (1 … 9, in the order painted) at any point: outside the painting it gives nothing, and a layer that wraps (one that drifts) is read round — with a shader its layers are not drawn by themselves, only as it reads them;
    emission (a vec3f it assigns; starts at 0) — light it adds unlit (a flame's heart: it blooms) — drawn where nothing is painted too (a glow over clear paint needs no paint under it);
    at_dx, at_dy (vec2f) — how far `at` moves one screen pixel across and one down (an edge one pixel soft: abs(at_dx) + abs(at_dy));
    u_time (f32) — the scene's seconds (for what goes round on and on — a twinkle, a flame — cycle(k): where in its cycle something going k times a second is now, 0..1, exact however long the scene stays open);
    u_<name> (f32) — one for each name of its "uniforms": { name: default } — 32 at the most; in the spec and in animate's e.uniform(name, value) the name stays as it is, in the text it is u_<name>;
    PI (f32); noise1(x: f32) -> f32, noise2(p: vec2f) -> f32, noise3(p: vec3f) -> f32 (smooth 0..1); hash2(p: vec2f) -> f32, hash3(p: vec3f) -> f32, hashU(x: u32) -> f32 (0..1); wobble(x: f32) -> f32, wobble2(p: vec2f) -> vec2f (−1..1); cycle(k: f32) -> f32; luma(c: vec3f) -> f32; srgbToLinear(c: vec3f) -> vec3f; wrap(x: f32, y: f32) -> f32 (x − y·floor(x / y): the remainder that never goes negative).
  WGSL is strict: every number that is not whole is written with its point (2.0); a parameter cannot be assigned (copy it into a var); `let` for what does not change, `var` for what does; a choice is select(ifNot, ifSo, condition), and both its sides are worked out; a vector is changed whole (c = vec4f(c.rgb + x, c.a)); one declaration a statement; braces round every body. No textures of its own, no discard, no derivatives (dpdx, dpdy, fwidth: at_dx and at_dy are there instead); loops with constant bounds. A name of yours may not begin with e_ or u_, nor be any of the names given above, and WGSL's own reserved words are not yours either (a uniform's name is letters, digits and _, beginning with a letter; a WGSL reserved word is fine there: it is u_<name> in the text). What it hides of its painting hides its glows (e.light) there too, as much as it hides. The scene's light, haze and shadows fall on what it returns; `emission` is added after the light. An error in it is the scene's error, naming the element and the line; so is layer(k) past 9, and more than 32 uniforms.
    "shader": "fn shade(painted: vec4f, at: vec2f, uv: vec2f) -> vec4f { let f = noise2(vec2f(at.x * 3.0, at.y * 2.0 + u_time * 2.5)); let c = layer(1, at + vec2f(0.0, f * 0.05)); emission = c.rgb * u_heat * 2.0; return c; }", "uniforms": { "heat": 1 }
  A painting with a shader is laid over its whole box, as see-through paint is — give it a box no bigger than it needs; where its painting (k 0) is opaque and stays opaque as it shades it, it hides what is behind it as any opaque paint does (water over the piles under it); what it paints over see-through paint stays see-through paint — over a see-through painting (a flame's) throughout. A layer(k, at) read at the shader's own `at` is as sharp as any painting; a painting read at another point (moved, tiled) is read whole, as a layer that drifts is, and is never sharper than about a screenful of pixels: keep such paintings small, or move the element instead of the read. Each different shader text is built when the scene opens: share one text among the elements that can (their uniforms tell them apart).
  The sky and an insert move the same way — layers, animate, shaders — in their stage heights: a moon is a layer its animate carries across the sky (its e.at inside the layer goes with it, and so does a light "from" it, its shadows too, in the same frame); the stars twinkle in the sky's shader; an insert's close-up cross-fades two layers by a key. Their e.light is a glow only (the sky's at infinity, an insert's in its frame) — it lights nothing in the world; a light from the sky is a far light "from": "moon#disc". A sky's e.point(ref) is where that point of the world is seen on the sky from the eye now (the moon rising out of a chimney: e.point('hut#chimney')); the points a sky names are the game's as anyone's ("moon#disc"): where they are seen on the screen now, as the sky is drawn — none while the eye looks away from them; a sky layer drifting all round a looking-around sky: give it "box": [0, top, e.aspect, bottom] so its ends meet. What a sky paints is drawn as any painting, as sharp as the screen needs; past it the sky carries on by itself — its top on up, its bottom on down, looking ahead its right end round behind into its left —, softly: a blur of the first shown sky element's edge, no detail. A phone held upright sees far above and below, a very wide screen far round, a camera turned (camera_yaw) or looking up farther: a scene that wants those to look right paints its sky farther — a "box" as wide and tall as they see (a whole turn across for a camera that turns all round; higher up for one that looks up) —, and leaves only what no screen sees to the carry. Let the edges of what it paints be the sky's colour there (a gradient's top, the horizon's haze), not a moon, a star or a cloud cut by the edge.
  Particles and the light in the air — snow, a blizzard, rain, smoke from a chimney, sparks off a hammer, a torch's fire and embers, dust kicked up by a landing, splashes, drops running down a window, fireflies, petals, leaves, bubbles; the haze glowing round a lamp, the sun's shafts through a window. All of it moves on the GPU by itself: nothing is painted per frame, nothing kept from frame to frame — any moment is drawn the same however it is reached (a render, a jump in the showcase).
  A particles element: { "id", "particles": { "kind", …its fields }, and where it is — "across", "distance", "elevation", "on", "place" as any element, or "at" in particles }. Its kind gives every field a value; give only what differs. Vectors are [across, distance, elevation] (metres, as "at" points are); sizes metres across; times seconds.

  Kinds (what each is as it starts — weather fills the air round the eye; the rest come out of where the element is):
    snow — flakes of many sizes falling slowly, fluttering, swirling, carried by the wind, settling on the ground a moment and gone. blizzard — snow driven hard by the wind, gusting, swirling: the whitened distance is the scene's air — give the look its haze and hazeColor (a storm rising: a look). rain — streaks falling fast, splashing where they land.
    drops — beads running down the element's face (a window pane: the element stands on it, facing as it does), each trailing a faint wet line, wandering a little.
    sparks — bursts of hot streaks flung up and out, falling, bouncing once off the ground, cooling from white-gold to red. embers — slow glowing motes rising from a fire, flickering, drifting with the air. fire — tongues of light rising and fading (with embers beside it and a light of its own in "lights": a flame lights nothing by itself).
    smoke — soft grey puffs rising from a point, widening and thinning as they widen (see-through), leaning with the wind, curling, fading away; darker on the side away from the light. puff — a burst of dust (or snow, flour, soot) kicked up and spreading low, settling. dust — fine motes hanging in the air of a room, lit only where a light falls (a sunbeam). splashes — a burst of droplets thrown up and falling back.
    fireflies — glowing points wandering, blinking. petals, leaves — tumbling down on the wind, settling on the ground. bubbles — rings rising and wobbling.
  Fields:
    Where they come from: "at" — a point they come out of: an element's ("chimney#mouth" — its e.at), a person's ("smith#hand_r": they follow the hand as it moves — what flies keeps where it was born), [across, distance, elevation], or {"on": id, "at": […]} (in an element's frame, going with it); without it, the element's own place. "source": [across, distance, elevation] — the size of the box round it they are born in (0: a point; [6, 6, 2]: fireflies over a meadow; [8, 0, 0]: a line — drips along an eave, the edge of what it stands "on", turned with it); "flat": true — born on the element's face (drops on a pane). "direction" — the way they set out (turned with the element), "spread" — degrees round it (180: every way), "speed": [min, max] m/s, "inherit" 0…1 — how much of the emitter's own motion they take (sparks off a swinging hammer).
    How many: "rate" — a second; "amount" — 0…1 or a state key: the share given now (a fire dying down, the snow beginning: what is already in the air goes on); "burst": { "on": a state key — each time it changes to something (a number up, true, a name) a burst goes off where the emitter is then; "count"; "over": seconds it is spread over } — or its "animate" says e.burst(count) (when a foot strikes the ground, every 0.6 s of a hammer); "life": [min, max] seconds each one lives.
    Weather round the eye ("around", the kinds snow, blizzard, rain): "count" — how many in the air at once round the eye, "reach" — metres it fills each way (what is farther is in the haze: the look's haze carries the storm on), "speed" — how fast they fall, "area": [across from, across to, distance from, distance to] — only there (the yard outside, not the room the eye is in); the eye walks through it, it never ends.
    How they fly: "gravity" — m/s² down (negative: it rises — smoke, embers, bubbles), "drag" — how soon the air stops them (a second's share: 0.3 a spark, 2 a puff of dust), "wind" — how much the scene's wind carries them (light "wind": [across, distance, strength], strength 1 ≈ 2 m/s of air; its looks change it — a storm getting up carries everything in the air with it), "gust" 0…1 — how much its gusts push them, "turbulence" — metres they swirl off their way, "swirl" — how big the swirls are.
    Where they land: "ground" — "pass" (through it), "stop" (lie there "settle" seconds, fading — snow, petals), "splash" (rain: a little crown for "settle" seconds), "bounce" (once, "bounce" 0…1 of their fall — sparks); on the land.
    How they look: "shape" — "dot", "flake" (round; smeared only by how it moves against the eye over a frame of the display: standing, round; flying past the eye, a short streak), "streak" (along its way: rain — "streak": the seconds of its way it shows), "spark" (a streak with a hot head), "puff" (a soft lump, each its own), "ring", "drop", "leaf", "petal" — or the element's "code" paints the sprite: once (again only when a key it reads changes), round its middle in its "box" (metres, [−0.5, −0.5, 0.5, 0.5] unless said), "frames": n of them side by side, each particle one of them (a curled leaf and a flat one, a snowflake's six shapes; it is drawn as long as the frame is wide). "size": [at birth, at the end] metres across, "spin" — degrees a second either way, "tumble": true — turning edge on and back (a leaf, a petal), "color": "#rrggbb" or [at birth, at the end] (a spark cooling), "colors": [up to 4] — each one of them, "opacity" 0…1, "fade": [in, out] — shares of its life it comes and goes over, "glow" — its own light, times its colour, [at birth, at the end] (above 1 it blooms: sparks 7, embers 4; 0: only lit), "blend": "add" (light added — sparks, fire, fireflies) or "over" (smoke, snow), "blink" 0…1 (fireflies), "flicker" 0…1 — how much each one's brightness wavers, lit or glowing.
    Light: they are lit as what faces no way of its own — a flake by the lamp bright, far from it as dark as the night; "sheer" 0…1 — light from behind comes through (smoke round the moon, rain against a street lamp); "inside": true (the element's) — lit as what is in a room. Hazed as anything is: far away, in the air's colour.
    "soft" — metres over which they fade into what stands behind them (smoke against a wall, no hard line).
    Shadows: they lie in the scene's shadows (smoke dark where the hut shades it from the moon, snow falling through a tree's shadow); "shadow" 0…1 — how much of the sun's or the moon's light they stop as much as they are there: smoke (0.6) and a cloud of dust (0.4) cast a soft moving shadow on what is under them and in the haze's shafts, and shade themselves (the far side of a column of smoke darker); thin things (snow, sparks, rain, motes) none.
  Order: particles are drawn among the paintings by their depth (a mist card before the smoke veils it; the smoke before the house covers it); each is soft against what stands behind it. Mirrors do not show them. The scene holds up to 65536 at once — more and every emitter gives that much less (said). A sprite larger than a couple of metres is see-through paint over the frame: a few tens of such at the most.
  The light in the air (light, its looks):
    "hazeGlow": 0… — the haze round every point light (a lamp, a fire, a window's e.light, lights' "reach") lit by it, seen through as much air as there is before what the eye meets: thick haze glows more, what stands before the lamp cuts its glow — roughly: a soft volume of light in the air, not a sharp one —, the ground under it ends it. A beam's (a light with "cone") is its cone in the air, as far as it reaches, broken where something it shines past casts a shadow. "glow" (a light's) — its halo; e.light — its own glow.
    "shafts": 0… — the far lights (the sun, the moon) scattering in the haze, brighter toward them: a glow about the moon in a misty night (1: thick air toward the moon about half as bright as snow it lights, away from it a tenth); beams where something shades them — through the panes of a light with "through" (the sun's shafts across a dusty room, the moon's through a church window), past what casts a shadow (the moon through bare branches, the sun past a hut's roof), the land, thick smoke; soft-edged, as wide beams of dusty light are (a window's glazing bars do not show in its beam). Give the haze too: no air, no shafts.
  Places, turns, tilts moved by the state (below) are motion too — the element's place moves, nothing is repainted.

  Something that LIES FLAT is a plane — a floor, a pier's deck, a table top, a chessboard, a rug, a road, a pond, the sea near you:
  { "id", "name", "plane": true, "x": 0..1 (or "across": metres), "distance": metres (to its centre), "elevation": metres (0 on the ground, 0.75 a table top), "width": metres (across), "depth": metres (away from you), "rotation": degrees about the vertical (default 0), "tilt": degrees its far edge rises (− falls away), "drape": true (lies over the "relief", below; a draped plane takes no "shader" — it is said once and not taken), "walk": true (people stand and walk on it: a bridge, a deck, a landing), "water": true (a water surface — a sea, a lake, a river —, lying level: the viewer's eye and the operator stay above it), "reflect": true | { "strength": 0..1 (0.35), "fade": metres (4; 0 never), "rough": 0..1 (0), "ripple": 0..1 (0) } — "strength", "rough" and "ripple" each a number or a state key (its value 0…1: the sea rougher as a storm's key rises), "color", "code", "animate", "shader" }
  Its code paints it seen FROM ABOVE, in metres, origin at its centre, x to the right, y TOWARD the viewer (a 8 m board spans x −4…4, y −4…4; its far edge is y −4). Paint it flat and true — squares square, planks straight; the engine lays it in perspective for wherever the viewer stands (never paint the perspective yourself). One plane may be as long as the place: a river or a road kilometres long is one plane — painted finer where it is seen close (don't cut it into pieces). Things standing on a plane are normal elements at their own distance; people stand on a plane that says "walk": true wherever it lies over them higher than the land (it may be "unseen": the floor of what a card paints). Its e.light glows where it lies. Whatever slopes is a plane with "tilt" (a roof's slope, a ramp, a stair; "rotation" 90 and "tilt": a deck's roll) — a card with "tilt" is what leans and swings (a lid, a board, a hatch). A plane shows its face to one before it: a roof's far slope seen from below is its back — hidden by what holds it up. "reflect": a mirror — a polished floor, still water, a wet deck, a wet roof, a mirror on a wall ("tilt": 90, turned by "rotation" to face the room; its frame painted on the wall behind): all before its face is seen in it mirrored about its true plane, as bright as it is. "fade": what is higher than that many metres over the mirror is not in it — a thing fades out with its height into the mirror's own paint, as into a fog, never darkened (a ceiling's reflection fades into the floor's paint); what it hides stays hidden, and the mirrored sky (always in it) shows where nothing mirrored stands before it; where nothing is mirrored the mirror shows its own paint. A mirror's image is a small, plain copy of the scene — the rougher the mirror, the coarser; soft by itself, never sharper —, without the particles; and a mirror seen in another mirror shows there as it is painted, not mirroring. "rough": water under a breeze, wet stone — what is mirrored is smeared along the mirror's way, and what shines is drawn out into a long path of light on it (give a sun or a moon painted in the sky an e.light); 0.3 a lake, 0.6 the open sea. "ripple": small waves going over it break the mirror and its paths into moving glints. An element that says "seen": "reflection" is seen only in mirrors — a ghost in the water: looking up, the window is empty; it casts no shadow, is never picked, and its own light lights only what the mirror shows. The other way round, a person no mirror shows (a ghost before the looking-glass): their key `<id>_reflected` false (people.md).

  The same thing many times — "copies": a forest, a fence's posts, a street's lamps, a fleet, a town's roofs from the air. A card or a plane (seen or unseen) with "copies": [ { "across", "distance", "elevation", "facing" and "roll" or "rotation", "tilt", "scale", "flip", "tint", "glow", "seed" }, … ] is painted once and stands in every place of the list: each copy where its "across" and "distance" say, as an element's do, with its own "elevation" and turn (left out: the element's) — a card's copy its own "facing", "tilt" and "roll", a plane's copy its own "rotation" and "tilt" (a gable roof's two slopes are one plane's two copies; the planks of a deck turned every way, the tiles of a floor) —, "scale" (0.25…4 of its size), "flip": true (mirrored left to right), "tint" (a color its colours are multiplied by), "glow" (how many times as bright its glows and its e.light shine), "seed" (the phase of its sway and its flicker — its number in the list unless said: neighbours never move in step). Each copy is lit, shadowed, hazed, hidden and pressed where it stands, on its own. A point of a copy is "id:k" (k from 0, the order of the list), "id:k#name", "id:k@"; the bare id is copy 0. The element's "animate" runs once and moves them all alike; its "place" is not taken — copies stand where the list says; nothing stands "on" a copy. Not for people, particles, the sky, inserts, a mirror ("reflect", "glass") or a draped plane. Many alike things are copies of a few painted kinds — about twenty — told apart by size, turn, flip and tint, never hundreds of separate paintings: each painting is painted before the scene opens and kept in memory.

  A form — a thing that is truly round or curved and is seen from more than one side: a hull, a rock, a vault, a column, a cask. A form is a card's or a plane's — not the sky's, an insert's, a draped plane's, a person's or an emitter's. In an element's code, beside its painting: e.form(fn, { "closed", "ground" }) — fn(u, v) returns the point [x, y, z] of the surface, in the element's own metres (x across, y UP, z toward its front), for u and v each going 0…1 across its painting: the painting's rectangle — its box — is the form unrolled, u along the box from left to right, v down it. "closed": "u", "v" or "both" — where the form meets itself (a cask all the way round): paint those two edges so that they meet. "ground": the colour under the paint (a mid grey unless said). The function is pure — it reads the state and the element's own numbers, nothing else; a form that reads a key is made again when the key changes, as a painting is; animate moves a form as it moves a card and cannot change its shape.
  Write the function yourself, or take a ready one — each of these returns such a function, spaced by length along the surface so that paint is not stretched one way; they are in the kit beside the world's modules (e.kit.bend…; kit.bend in the lib). For each, how the painting's rectangle lies on the form — paint the box in those proportions:
  - e.kit.bend(width, height, degrees, about) — a sheet `width` by `height` metres bent into an arc of `degrees` (90 unless said; above 0 its middle comes toward the front, below 0 it goes away). About the vertical (the default: a curved wall, a tower's side, a sail) u goes round the arc and v down it; with about: "across" it is bent about the level line (a vault, an awning): v goes round the arc, u along it. The painting is the sheet unrolled, width by height, and lies on it unstretched; the middle of its foot is the element's anchor.
  - e.kit.revolve(profile) — a thing turned on a lathe (a column, a cask, a vase, a dome): profile — [[radius, height], …] from its top down, metres. u goes once round it — the painting's middle on its front, its two side edges meeting at the back (say "closed": "u") —, v down the profile by its length: the painting is the way round by the profile's length. Where the radius comes to 0 the paint is pinched to a point.
  - e.kit.extrude(profile, path) — a cross-section carried along a way (a moulding, a rail, a gutter, a keel): profile — [[x, y], …] in the section's own plane (x to its right, y up, as one looks along the way), path — [[x, y, z], …] metres. u goes along the profile by its length, v along the path by its length: the painting is the section's length by the way's length.
  - e.kit.loft(ribs) — a skin over ribs (a hull over its frames, a tent over its poles, a trunk): ribs — [[[x, y, z], …], …], two or more, each a line of points in the element's metres, all running the same way. u goes along each rib by its length, v from the first rib to the last by the way between them: the painting is a rib's length by the way from rib to rib (a hull unrolled: keel at one edge, rail at the other, bow at one end).
  - e.kit.lump(outline, height, seed) — a mound (a rock, a drift, a heap): outline — [[x, z], …] its rim on the level, metres (z toward the front), going round counter-clockwise as seen from above (x to the right, z toward the viewer): the painting then lies on its outside unmirrored; `height` — its top over the rim; `seed` — which uneven mound of that outline it is (0: smooth). u goes once round the rim by its length (say "closed": "u"), v from its top (0) down to its rim (1): the painting is the way round by the slope from top to rim; its steep sides stretch the paint.
    code: g.fillStyle = '#8a8478'; g.fillRect(-3.1, -4, 6.2, 4); …the stone's courses and stains over the box [-3.1, -4, 3.1, 0]…; e.form(e.kit.revolve([[0.9, 4], [1, 3.8], [1, 0.2], [1.1, 0]]), { closed: 'u' });
  A world's module named as one of the five is not seen under that name.
  A form is opaque: where you paint nothing its ground colour shows, never the world behind. It is lit by the scene as a volume — each part by how it is turned to each light — so its painting carries colour and pattern, not light and shade. It hides itself and what is behind it truly from every side, casts its shadow, is mirrored and pressed like anything, and its e.at(x, y, name) points lie ON it (people look at them and reach for them, lights and particles stand on them). The engine chooses how finely it is drawn — every form the same, whatever its function: a form that fills the frame shows an outline of short straight pieces, and where a surface curves two ways at once (a pole, a steep side) its paint is stretched.
  An element with a form has one painting and none of: layers, parts, a shader, "blend", "translucent", "reflect", "glass", "wind", "drift", "clip" — any of them is the scene's error. "copies" are fine. People do not stand on a form: they stand on planes and the land — in a boat, on a deck plane inside the hull (it may be "unseen"), and the hull hides their legs at the rail by itself.
  Whether a thing should be a form at all is yours to weigh: a round thing far off, or seen from one side only, is still a painting on a flat sheet; a form is for what turns in view or is walked round. Tens of forms in a view, not hundreds.

A widget is a piece of interface over the scene — a sign over someone's head, a clock, a letter, a note, a map, a scoreboard, a panel with buttons — when the world needs one; most scenes need none:
  { "id", "name", "html": string,       // its HTML with <style> and <script> inside: HTML, CSS, JavaScript — anything a web page can do
    "width": px, "height": px,          // its size, in px of a 1080-px-tall screen (it is scaled with the screen)
    "x": 0..1, "y": 0..1,               // where it stands in the frame (interface: x 0.02 is the left edge on any screen) ...
    "follow": element id, "lift": metres, // ... or it hovers over that element (lift metres above its anchor; "id#name" — over a point it names) and moves with it as the viewer walks
    "anchor": "center"|"top"|"bottom"|"left"|"right"|"top-left"|…,  // which point of it sits there (default center; bottom when following)
    "interactive": boolean,             // takes clicks (default false: clicks go through to the scene)
    "at": seconds, "for": seconds }     // appears later / for a while, like captions
  Its markup and <style> live in their own shadow root (its CSS and the page's don't mix; the background is transparent). Its scripts run in the page, in order, with `root` — that shadow root: find its parts with root.getElementById / root.querySelector, not document — and `widget`, its element. Style it to belong to the scene (its palette and light), small and quiet. A widget is a layer of the page: it is always over the whole picture — nothing of the scene can stand in front of it. What must be hidden by things of the scene, or stand among them, is an element of the scene, not a widget.

State — a scene's values, and what it does with them:
  "state" holds named values and where they start: the time of night, the dawn 0..1, a lamp lit, where someone stands, what they are doing, what has happened. Painting reads them as e.state (e.state.dawn) and is painted again when one it read changes — so the picture follows the state; animate reads them every frame. The game played on the scene turns these values as its story goes (a girl who looks up, a door that opens, the light going out): give the scene the ones its story could need — who is there and what they can do, the light, what can happen — and paint every value well. A scene opens in the state it is opened with: nothing is painted for a state nobody sees.
  Describe the values in "states", so whatever turns them — the game, its agents, another artist — knows what they can be without reading your code: each key — "range": [min, max] (a number), or "values": [what it can be] ("shut", "ajar", "open"), or "type": "number" | "boolean" | "string"; "smooth": true — painted at every value between (a door at 0.37 is partly open), moved over time; "means": what it shows ("0 — the door shut, 1 — wide open"). A key set from outside that nothing in the scene knows or reads, or a value outside its description, is said in a render's warnings — so give every key of your own (the camera_… are the engine's).

Scripts and the director — the scene is driven by events, not by seconds.
  A "script" — of the scene or of any element — is JS run with the scene, in its own thread (no document, no window, no painting). It gets `scene`, and starts each time the scene opens, with the state already restored: write it to go on from the state, not from the beginning (a clock: every 20 s add ten minutes to state.time). Scripts are the scene's mechanics only: they know nothing of the game played on it — no one is made to speak for the story, nothing is decided for the player; clicks and talk belong to the game. Widgets use the global MooodState (.get, .set, .on, .all(), .states — the scene's "states").
  scene.state (current values), scene.get(key), scene.time (seconds since the scene opened), scene.view (what the screen shows: above), scene.was(key, secondsAgo), scene.ground(across, distance), scene.random() (0..1, the same every time the scene is played — never Math.random), scene.anim, scene.kit.<module>, scene.lib, scene.tell(text, about?) (says something happened — a ship came round the cape: the game hears it), scene.warn(text).
  Actions — everything that takes time:
  - scene.person(id): .walk([[across, distance], …], {speed, gait, steps, queue}) — along the points from where they stand (their place's keys), facing the way they go; without a speed at their gait's own pace (its motion at its real rate carries them that far a second: a child's shorter stride is slower — never faster steps; another speed is another gait, a run, made as such); steps: false — moved as they are, sliding; .go(motion, {to | from: [across, distance], turn, speed}) — a motion done once that ends at `to` (or begins at `from`) facing `turn`: they walk to where it begins, turn, and do it; .do(motion, {loop, from, going}) — a motion done once takes as long as it does (never sped up or slowed to fit); looped, it is what they do from now: done at once; .turn(degrees) — as a person turns; .say(text, {voice, how}) — the line over them, heard in their voice (voice: its name; how: the manner, in English — a line in its own manner says its own; inline tags in the text — <sigh>, <short pause> — heard, not shown), as long as its voice (read, when there is none); and, at once: .look(at), .reach({r, l, foot_r, foot_l}), .with(parts), .wear(fields), .gait(motion), .on(point), .here(yes), .still(on), .reflected(yes), .mood(expression), .talk(on) (people.md); .startOf(motion, turn) — where a motion done once begins, from where it ends (scene.startOf(id, motion, turn): the same, by the person's id);
  - scene.set(key, value, over?, ease?) — at once, or a number moved `over` seconds along an easing curve ('smooth' by default; 'linear', 'in', 'out', 'inOut', 'back', 'spring', 'anticipate'); scene.move(key, value, {over, ease, queue}); scene.turn(key, degrees, over?, ease?) — an angle the short way round; scene.path([keys], points, {speed | over, ease}) — several numbers moved together through points as one movement (someone along a path, a cart on a road: [['cart_a', 'cart_d'], [[2, 8], [5, 12], [9, 13]]]; it slows nowhere but where its curve says; its way is measured in metres along the keys that place something (across, distance, elevation; camera_x, camera_h, camera_z): `speed` is metres a second; the others it moves (a facing, a turn, any number) follow along, adding nothing to the way — a point that moves only them is passed at once (a turn on the spot is scene.turn, over seconds); a path of other numbers alone is measured in them;
  - scene.camera({target, shot, frame, glide}) — the operator frames what it is told (camera_target …), done once it has settled; scene.light(id).fade(to, {over, ease}) — a light's `on` moved; scene.say(id, text, opts) — a line said by anyone (a narrator, a thing). Every text a person reads or hears is written in English, as a literal, beside whose it is — s.person('id').say('…'), scene.say('id', '…'); what is painted or shown, tr('…') (tr('{n} miles', { n }): its values by name) — it is translated beside the scene (Translations, below).
  Each returns an action: `await a` (it ends: 'done', 'cancelled' or 'replaced'), `await a.ending(1)` (a second before it ends — once its length is known: a line's when its voice says; at once if it is shorter), a.cancel(), a.pause() / a.resume() (the process stops, not time: a walk paused stands where it is, a motion paused holds its pose), a.finish() (done at once, as it would end), a.remaining (seconds, as known or estimated), a.duration (null while not known).
  Tracks — each actor has its own: a person's body (walk, go, do, turn), a person's voice, each key, the camera. A new action on a track replaces the one there and goes on smoothly from wherever it is — a walk from where they stand now, a key from its value at the speed it was moving (unless you give an ease: then from rest); {queue: true} waits its turn instead. Different tracks run side by side: she walks while the camera follows and the lamp dims. A value set at once stops what was moving it.
  Sequences — what happens, as code that waits on what happens: scene.sequence(async s => { … }, {name}) — s has all of scene's above (its actions this sequence's own), and: s.wait(seconds) — a pause, only where time truly is; s.until(key | () => condition | action) — till a key is true, a condition holds, an action ends; s.event(name) — till the game (MooodState.signal(name, data)) or a script (scene.signal(name, data)) says so: its data; s.all(a, b, …), s.any(a, b, …, {cancel: true}) — all of them, the first of them (the others stopped); s.sequence(fn) — one inside it; branch with plain if/else on the state. A named sequence replaces the one running by that name. seq.cancel() stops it and all it started (every wait in it throws Cancelled: use try/finally to put things right); seq.pause() pauses it and all it started. scene.after(seconds, fn), scene.every(seconds, fn) — on the scene's clock. A game may jump to a moment of itself (every action finished at once, a sequence's waits with them, then the scene shown once): end sequences by what happens, never by waiting in a loop — what repeats on the clock is scene.every.
    scene.sequence(async s => {
      const vakula = s.person('vakula'), oksana = s.person('oksana');
      await vakula.walk([[1.2, 6.5]]);
      const line = s.person('vakula').say('Oksana!');   // (a line: whose it is written beside it)
      await line.ending(0.8);            // she turns a moment before he ends
      await oksana.turn(200);
      if (s.state.mood === 'cold') await s.person('oksana').say('What do you want?');
      else await s.all(oksana.do('smile'), vakula.do('bow'));
      await s.event('next');             // the game's word
    });
  Events — scene.on(type, [who | key,] fn): 'change' (fn(value, old, key): a value set — moved: where it goes, once), 'move' (each frame while it moves), 'load', 'action' ({ id, actor, verb, ev: started | ending | done | cancelled | replaced | paused | resumed }), 'arrived' ({who}: a walk's end), 'line' ({who, text, ev: start | heard | end, duration}), 'cut', 'signal' ({name, data}), 'tell'.
  The game drives the scene the same way, from the page: MooodState.act(actor, verb, …args) → an action (actor: a person's id; 'state' — set, move, turn, path; 'camera' — frame, cut; 'light:<id>' — fade); MooodState.signal(name, data); MooodState.on('arrived' | 'line' | 'cut' | 'signal' | 'tell', [who,] fn). The scene can be saved at any moment and restored (the state, the actions running, what animate keeps, the camera, the people, what is in the air) — sequences are code, not data: the scripts start again, from the state.

The showcase — "showcase": the body of a function (scene) returning the scene's own small scenario: what it shows outside games (the scene's page, the preview), played like a film with a timeline (it plays from the scene's first frame; its length is an estimate until it has been played through once, then known). Its beats, one after another — [async s => …, …] or [{ name, run: async s => … }, …] — show all the scene can do: its people's doing, its light turning, its changes, its camera's shots. Each beat starts from what the beats before left: a jump back lands at a beat's start and plays on to the moment unseen, a jump ahead plays unseen from where the film is — keep in the state what a beat needs; everything there is exactly as played — only people's loose cloth, their faces and what springs are at rest in the pose, as after a cut. A beat is as long as what happens in it. Chance from scene.random(); nothing waits for what only a game sends (s.event) — it plays by itself; a line's length is estimated till its voice is heard. Lines said (scene.say, act(id, 'say', …)) belong to the showcase only — please do not say lines from a scene's own always-running scripts or sequences: they would speak over a game that has its own words and voices, and the game cannot tell them apart from its own. Give every scene with something to show a showcase of a minute or two; in a game it never runs.
    "showcase": "return [
      { name: 'evening', run: async s => { await s.move('dusk', 1, { over: 6 }); } },
      { name: 'he comes', run: async s => { await s.person('vakula').walk([[0, 5]]); await s.person('vakula').say('Good evening.'); } },
      { name: 'night', run: async s => { const next = s.shot({ camera_target: 'window', camera_shot: 'close' }); await s.wait(2); next.cut(); await s.light('lamp').fade(0, { over: 3 }); } },
    ];"

The camera a film moves, through state values:
  - camera_target: an element's id ("id#name" — a point it names) — an operator frames it and keeps it framed as it moves, room ahead the way it goes, setting off and settling gently, easing from one target to the next; null — back to the scene's own camera. Its point is its e.at (a head), else its box's middle; a point it names and does not mark now (an animate's, gone a while) — the operator holds where it framed it; a name the thing does not mark at all (none in its painting, none its animate has marked) is said (a warning), and the camera stays as it was; a plane's point (a spot on the square, "square#well") is framed as if one stood there — a person's height. Not the sky, an insert or particles. It may be off the frame (the operator turns to it) or "unseen" (a point to look at: between two people). camera_shot: "wide" (a third of the frame), "full" (default), "medium" (waist up), "close" (head and shoulders), or how many times the frame's height it is tall — the target element's own height, whatever point of it is named ("bench#seat" at "medium" frames the bench, not those sitting on it: the lens a step from the seat, among their knees); a two-shot's point between people is an "unseen" element as tall as they are ("height": 1.7) or a plane's point; camera_frame: where its point goes, [x, y] fractions of the frame (x alone: across); camera_glide: seconds to get there (1.5; 0 — at once). The operator's eye is at eye height over what the target stands on — level with a deck, with the back of the elephant it rides — and comes up to the target's point as the shot closes in (medium, close); over a "water" plane it may stand, the eye above the water; gliding from one place to another it goes level, as it goes along (off a quay onto a bridge's road it does not dip to the river between); a person not here (`<id>_here` false) is not framed. Ahead the operator dollies and slides, from the viewer's side — a face turned away from the start is its back; frame it yourself (camera_x, camera_z, camera_yaw), or film the scene looking around. Looking around it turns and comes as near as the shot needs: a person it films from before their face (as they face when the shot begins), a thing from where the viewer is; and it never shoots through what stands between the lens and the target: as a shot begins, if a person or something truly painted hides most of the target (a veil — thin mist, smoke, light added — hides nothing), it goes round to the nearest bearing that sees it, then nearer, within the scene's area (no such bearing in the area: from where the viewer is); a bearing whose lens would be inside a person's body — another's or the target's own (a giant on a throne, its knees toward the lens) — is not taken. How near it comes is the shot's size from the target's point, whatever the target's bulk; ahead (below) nothing keeps the lens out of a body: a figure far bigger than a person, sitting, crouching or lying, may reach past that distance toward the lens — frame such a one wider, or move the camera yourself (camera_z, camera_x). Ahead (a panorama, a walk, a picture) it steps round nothing: what stands between stays in the way — the camera is yours to choose (camera_x, camera_z, camera_h, camera_yaw, camera_frame), or move what is in the way. A person framed whom something nearer — another person, what is painted (not a veil), a form, a rail — hides by more than a third is said as the shot settles (a warning: the scene's time, who, and what hides them).The viewer's own moves go on top — a look aside, easing back once they let go, never nearer than half the way to the target.
  - camera_on: an element's id — the camera rides that one (as "camera".on); false — none: it stands in the world, at the start (the ship seen passing from the shore); null — as the scene's camera says. Where the camera is, is said in the frame of what it rides, so riding something else is a new shot: set it by a cut (s.shot({camera_on: false, camera_target: "ship", camera_shot: "wide"}) … next.cut()) — the viewer at the start there, the operator on its target at once. A cut that moves what it rides takes the camera along.
  - camera_x (metres right), camera_h (metres up — the target stays framed), camera_z (metres ahead — a dolly in, with all the scene's depth), camera_look (screen heights the gaze is lowered): added on top of the framing or the scene's camera. They start at 0, and stay as set when camera_target changes: a dolly's camera_z left from before carries the new target's framing as far (past the target, to where it is not) — give a target shot the offsets it wants (a cut's values, or moved back to 0).
  - The camera turned, degrees, on top of all that: camera_pitch (up; −75…75 — the lens shifted: what stands stays upright), camera_yaw (to the right, all the way round in any camera: looking ahead, what stands in the world is seen truly from any side; the sky is carried round as a backdrop — a scene to be seen all round paints its sky and its far land for around/roam), camera_roll (the picture tipped, the horizon rising to the right: a banked turn, a Dutch angle; it comes a little closer to fill its corners). Move them as any number — set at once the camera is there at once, which is a new shot, shown whole like a cut: so move it, and set it at once only to cut.
  A scene never fades itself and has no fade of its own (a "fade" in its "state" is not the engine's: told so, nothing drawn): a scene at rest is always seen (its first frame, its pictures and previews too). Transitions through black, a dim under a letter, black at the end are the game's and the page's (the stage's fade over the scene), not the scene's.
  Cuts — a new shot is always shown whole: never the new framing with pictures still coming, never blurred and sharpening; so a cut needs no fade to hide work. A cut is scene.cut({camera_target: "keeper", camera_shot: "close", keeper_a: 2, …}): a new shot, its values at once (people placed, the camera there, what came before forgotten: e.cut, the viewer's moves undone) — the values are set that moment, as scripts expect; the picture follows when it is whole. Whether that is at once depends on you: say the next shot before you cut to it — const next = s.shot({…all the values the cut will set…}) (scene.shot the same) — and the engine makes it ready meanwhile: its view, and what its values paint anew (a face for the close-up, another ship's rig). A shot sees what its values place or show by the keys the engine reads — an element's place, a person's, a layer shown by its key; what an element's "animate" shows by a key of the cut it sees only after the cut, and the cut is held for those pictures. Then, when it is time, next.cut(): the new shot is there at once, finished. next.ready — true once it is (at once where nothing is drawn: a moment sought, a render); it always comes, whatever the camera rides; a story that must not go on before it waits so: await s.until(() => next.ready) — the scene plays on meanwhile, and a wait long enough to notice is shown by the page's sign of waiting; next.cancel() — not wanted after all. A shot has no seconds: it lives until it is cut to or cancelled (one made inside a sequence is cancelled with the sequence); each maker — a sequence, the scene's scripts, the page — keeps two at the most: a third lets its oldest go, and that one's cut is then a plain cut. A cut nobody said ahead — or one cut to before it was ready — still shows its shot whole: the story waits for it — the scene's scripts, its film, its lines stand (what they do after the cut is done after it; what they read meanwhile is the state before it) — while the last picture lives on (wind, particles, people breathing), under the page's sign of waiting once it lasts, until the new one is ready: a moment for a framing of things already seen, longer where much must be painted. So: wherever a cut can be known, say its shot; what a script shows without a cut (a layer shown by a key, a thing coming into view) is never waited for — it comes in a moment, soft first: make it a shot if it must appear whole; a film's module that knows its next beat says that beat's opening. A camera that follows something moves over time (an action, a path), not by values set at once frame after frame. Moving past near things is fine: a thing that stands (a card) fades only in the last 60…20 cm before the eye (a curtain of leaves walked through); a plane, the land and a form do not fade.
  An insert — a shot of its own over the scene, fixed to the frame whatever the camera does (a hand close up, a letter, a face): an element with "insert": a state key opening it, 0 (closed — not drawn) … 1 (open). Its code paints in stage heights like the sky (x 0..e.aspect, y 0..1; paint its own background). It is seen through an iris: "iris": [x, y, rx, ry] — the window fully open, an oval (centre and radii, stage heights), opening from its middle; without it, from the middle of the frame to all of it. It is laid over the safe frame and painted as wide as the widest (x (e.aspect − 7/3)/2 .. (e.aspect + 7/3)/2, y 0..1); a screen wider than that, or taller than the stage, sees the scene going on round it. The scene goes on under it; closing it is the way back. No haze, no camera, no depth, none of the world's light — it is a painted picture laid flat over the frame, and carries its own painted light; it is over everything — "behind", "inFront" and "clip" neither name it nor are its. A closed insert is made ready by the engine: it opens finished the moment its key leaves 0 (a moment later where the view on screen fills the engine's memory — never unfinished) — it is not a shot to say ahead. The points it names (e.at, in its code or its animate) are the game's as anyone's: where it draws them on the screen now — only while it is open and they are within its iris as far as it is open (closed: none); its id alone is its own e.at, else its iris's centre. Sixteen inserts a scene at the most.
  An element that walks off into the distance or comes near has "place": its across, distance, elevation (or x, bearing) are the names of state values (metres) — the engine puts it there every moment: its size, its haze, what it hides and what hides it follow; on a "relief", over the land — up a bank, down to the water. "facing", "tilt" and "roll" too (degrees; a plane's "rotation" and "tilt" too: a deck rolling): a door or a window leaf opens — the card turns about its anchor, so paint it from its hinge (x 0 → its width; a leaf hinged on the right: x −width → 0): "facing": 180, "place": {"facing": "door"}, door 180 shut → 262 open into the room (the free edge comes toward the viewer: + for a card painted to the right of its hinge, − for one painted to the left); move it with scene.move or scene.turn. Two leaves: two keys moved together.
  "on": "house" — it stands on another element and goes and turns with it: its "across", "distance", "elevation" are metres from that one's anchor along its own axes, as if it faced the start (across to its right, up, away behind its face; on a plane: across it, up from its face, toward its far edge), and its "facing", "tilt", "roll" turn within that one's. A thing built of cards is so assembled on an unseen anchor and turned as a whole by one number:
  {"id": "house", "unseen": true, "across": -6, "distance": 12, "facing": 200}, {"id": "front", "on": "house", "facing": 180, …}, {"id": "side", "on": "house", "across": 3, "facing": 90, …}, {"id": "roof_front", "plane": true, "on": "house", "elevation": 3.8, "distance": 1.25, "tilt": 32, …}
  — "place": {"facing": "house_turn"} on the anchor turns it all; a ship heels the same way ("tilt"). A chain carries along; what stands on something is not set on the relief. A card facing the eye on something that tilts leans with it, its face toward the start (where the scene's camera starts on what it rides, "camera".on) — the one the camera rides itself has no way toward the start (the eye stands on it): it stands on what carries it facing the way that one faces, as it would with "facing": 180, and the camera looks the way "distance" grows on it. People stand on what carries them upright (a deck rolling under their feet) and move in its frame: their place and turn are in it — only those walk and turn them (a carriage at speed walks no one; a ship turning turns them with it). A point that goes with something — out of a carriage's window, where a watch lies on a palm in it — is {"on": id, "at": [across, distance, elevation]}: in its frame as "on" places things; it is taken wherever a point is (a person's look, reach, a seat; particles' "at"; e.point, e.where). Not on the sky, an insert, a person or a copy.

Light — the scene's light is state, and it is the engine's: every scene is lit by it.
  - "looks": each key names a state value 0…1 turning the light toward that look — { "evening": {"ambient": 0.6, "ambientColor": "#ffb070", "tint": "#ff9050", "tintStrength": 0.15}, "night": {"ambient": 0.18, "ambientColor": "#5070c0", "saturation": 0.6, "haze": 0.5} }: scene.move('night', 1, {over: 20}) brings the night in over 20 s; looks apply in the order listed. Any field of "light" may be in a look ("wind" too: a storm rising), and "lights": { light id: how much of its strength } — {"morning": {…, "lights": {"moon": 0}}}: the moon's light goes out as the morning comes, with its shadows ("on" still multiplies); the sky element paints its own (let it read e.state.night too).
  - The engine lays its light on what is painted, by each thing's distance from each source: where none reaches, a thing keeps "ambient" of its colour (times "ambientColor"; 1 unless said — a scene with no lights and no "ambient" shows its paintings as painted); near a lamp it is lit full. What faces a way — planes, cards with "facing", forms — is lit by how it is turned to each source ("from": the sun on the faces turned to it, the shaded ones dim): a lamp's pool rounds over a side wall, the ceiling and the floor; a window's light falls on the walls by their turn. A card with no "facing" (a figure, a tree, a front painted flat) is rounded toward the eye: a lamp before it lights it full, one beside it half, one behind it little (whoever stands before the lamp is a silhouette). The sky and "glows" elements keep their colours; glows bloom. Light bounces: what lies in a shadow or is turned from the light is filled by what is lit about it (light "bounce", 0.5); 0 for a stark, black-shadowed look. There is one light for everything: people, things and the land are lit by the same sources, the same way — a scene does not paint a light of its own onto its paintings and does not light its people apart.
  - "lights" — any number of them: every entry, and every e.light, is a light, and none is refused — a street of 24 lamps, each its own pool; a town of a thousand lit windows. The engine chooses, each moment, which of them light what is in view (those that reach it most; a far small one begins to light things once its pool is a few pixels wide) — the glow of every one is always drawn. A point { "id", "across" (or "x"), "distance", "elevation" (metres), "place" (a lamp carried) — or "at": a point it goes with — a person's ("keeper#hand_r": a lantern in their hand, going where the hand goes; not while they are away; "keeper": their hands), an element's ("cart#lamp", as its painting or its animate marks it; "cart:2#lamp" — that copy's; an "unseen" element's point too) —, "color", "strength" (1), "reach" (metres, 8), "on": state key (shines that much, 0…1), "flicker": 0…1, "glow": metres (a halo the engine draws) }; an element's own light taken over { "id", "of": "lamp" (its e.light; "lamp#wick" — one it names; of an element with copies, every copy's — "lamp:2", "lamp:2#wick": that copy's alone, before what names them all), and any of "color", "strength", "reach", "on", "flicker" } — it shines from wherever the element paints it, its "on" putting out its glow too; or far off { "id", "from": [bearing, height] (degrees: 0 the far side, 90 the right, 180 behind the viewer; above the horizon) — "place": {"bearing": key, "height": key} moves it — or "from": "moon#disc", a point a sky element marks (e.at): it shines from wherever the moon is painted now, "color", "strength", "on" } — the moon, the sun; with "through": [pane, …] — each a window element's id (its rectangle) or a flat polygon of [across, distance, elevation] points (metres; the elevation over the land there, as everywhere) — it comes in only through them, its edge "soft" (metres, 0.3): the sun's patch on the floor, cast along the beam and up a wall; its shafts across a gallery, lighting who stands in them — what is before them all (the yard the window looks out on) as any light far off. A beam — a lighthouse, a searchlight, a car's headlamp, a stage spot — is a point light (any of those three) with "cone": { "bearing" (degrees: 0 the far side, 90 the right — as "from"), "height" (degrees above the level; below 0 it points down), "width" (its full angle, degrees, 1…170; 30), "soft" (0…1: how much of its edge fades; 0.25) }: it lights only within its cone, and its "glow" shines full only toward an eye inside it. A lighthouse's beam is the lantern's own light: give the lantern element an e.light where the lamp is and take it over — "of": "lighthouse#beam" with the cone —; an element never shades a light it carries (its own e.light, one taken over, one "at" a point of it, a lantern in a person's hand), so the tower does not hide its lamp. It turns only by keys: "place": {"bearing": key, "height": key} (degrees; any number of turns) — a lighthouse's endless turn is a script moving its key on; it shadows as a lamp does. Its light thins with the square of the distance, as a real beam's: "strength" is how strong it is a tenth of its "reach" away (nearer, what it lights is lit no more than that); what it lights takes it by how squarely it faces it (a beam skimming the sea lights it thinly). In a haze that glows ("hazeGlow") its cone is seen in the air with the same strength — brightest by the lantern, a long shaft fading out; a stronger beam, or a thicker haze, shows more. Of the far lights eight light at once at the most, of the through-lights four — the strongest —, of the beams two — those that light the view most; more is said.
  - Inside and outside: a room, a carriage, a cabin is darker than the world beyond its window. Mark what is in it "inside": true (its walls, floor, things, people) — a person not marked so is inside while what they stand on is: the "walk" floor under them, or what carries them ("on"), marked "inside" — so one walking in from the yard is lit as the room is from the step onto its floor, and as the yard is again outside; one marked "inside" is inside wherever they stand —, and give "light" an "inside": 0…1 — how much of the light outside reaches in (0.3: a room by day as dim against the window as it should be; in a look too). The light coming in at a window is a light with "daylight": true, or how strong (1) — its colour and strength are the outside's "ambient" now (the sky seen through it: day, dawn, a moonlit night), and it lights only what is inside. Give it "of": the window element and it comes in at all of the window: into the room only, the way the window's face looks, strongest straight before it and less aslant. Without "of" it is a point where you put it. The sun and the moon come in as the "inside" share, unless they come in "through" the windows: then only there. Lamps light both.
  - "shadow": the element — or a plane: a table top, a roof's slope — casts its own painting, where the light throws it — onto "ground" (what people stand on: the land as it rises and falls, the planes they walk on or are carried by — a deck, a stage —, a plane lying level on the ground), a plane (a floor, a table top) or an upright element (a wall): a shadow runs along the floor and climbs the wall. The shadows of one light are one shadow, whoever casts them. Which lights cast is the engine's choice — four at once at the most: first the lights a caster's "light" names (a source's id), then the far ones (the sun, the moon, a light "through" windows), then the strongest by what casts in view; as the view changes a lamp's shadow may pass to a stronger lamp. "strength" (0.5): how dark in full light — less by the caster's "translucent"; casters of unlike strength near each other cast alike. All shadows are of one softness. A shadow lies sharp on what is opaque and faces a way — the ground, a plane, a card with "facing", a form — from the two lights that matter most to the view, and softly, as a dimming, on anything else and from the others. A shadow has the shape of the painting a little coarser than the painting is seen: paint a few pixels thin (ropes, twigs) casts thin or none. What only sways (wind, drift, shimmer, a shader) casts as it stands at rest. Keep the lamp outside the casting element (the lights an element paints, and those of what it stands on, cast none of its shadows; nothing casts on itself). Parts and layers cast from where each is. What is out of the frame casts still; what a clip hides casts none. People cast and receive shadows by themselves ("shadow": false — a person casting none), and the ground is a little darker right under them, whatever lights them. A few hundred casters a scene.
  - "translucent": what is thin lets the light behind it through — lit from behind it glows its own colour that much (a sail against the low sun, leaves, a paper lantern, a curtain at a window), and its shadow is that much lighter (lighter, not coloured). A wall painted flat is a card as a sail is; only you know which is thin.
  - Accepted, and doing nothing in engine 3 — do not write them in a new scene: a caster's "soft" ("shadow": { "soft" }); a light's "size"; a mirror's and a pane's "blur" ("reflect": { "blur" }, "glass": { "blur" }); a plane's "lit". (A through-light's "soft" and particles' "soft" are other words, and work.)

Space — what is in front of what, the land, parts, points:
  The world is 3D: what is nearer hides what stands behind it, pixel by pixel, wherever the viewer goes (a fence aslant and someone by it, a table and the wall); what a card paints below its foot lies on the ground toward the eye. When that is not what is meant, say so instead of tuning distances (4.68 against 4.95): "behind": ["table"] — drawn behind those, "inFront": ["rock"] — in front of them.
  "clip": {"inside": ["doorway"]} — seen only where those elements are painted now (as they stand in the frame): a cat painted behind a pane, a cart going in under an arch, a lantern's glint in a puddle; {"outside": ["table_top"]} — only where they are not: a cloth's folds below a table top, a boat's hull under the water. "clip" is for cards and planes: a person is neither clipped nor clips. A shape painted only for this is an element with "unseen": true: placed and painted like any other, never shown, never picked. A scene has four different lists of such elements at the most (the same list named by many is one) — a fifth is said and clips nothing; a plane draped on the land does not clip.
  "glass": a pane of glass — a window at night, a train's window, a shop window: it paints only where the glass is (the opening, plain fill; frames and bars are another element over it — "behind": ["wall"]), and is not shown itself: in it, faintly ("strength"), what is on the viewer's side of it is seen mirrored — the room, lit as it is, its lamps. It shows where it is dark beyond the glass and light before it: at night; by day hardly. With "facing" it lies as that turns it; else across the gaze. The panes of one wall are one mirror; two mirroring planes and one such wall in a view at the most — each draws the scene again, small.
  "relief" — the land: the body of function (across, distance, anim, lib) returning the ground's height in metres there (across: right of the start's line of sight; distance: ahead). A bank rising to 1.4 m at 10 m and down to a stream at 14 m: "return 1.4 * anim.span(distance, 6, 10) - 1.8 * anim.span(distance, 11, 14)". Keep it 0 where the viewer starts. It is asked for every point of the land in view — thousands of times a frame as the view moves: plain arithmetic on its numbers — it makes nothing (no array, object or destructuring in it; what is worked out once is in the lib). Then:
  - what is placed by its "distance" stands on it (its elevation over the ground there), and one moving ("place") goes over it: down the bank to the water is a walk, not a slide down the screen;
  - a card that is a picture of the land itself (a bank painted side-on, the stones and sand on it, drawn in the land's own metres) says "onLand": false — it stands at its elevation, not lifted onto the relief it draws;
  - a plane with "drape": true lies on it like a cloth: paint the land as such planes (a field, a bank, a road), its slopes true from anywhere — laid as finely as the land bends and as it is seen; what is sheer (a cliff's edge returning one height on one side and another on the other) the land does not draw: a card for the cliff's face stands on that line; lit as each slope faces the light, shadows lying along the slope they fall on; other planes stay flat (water, a table, a floor) — keep the land under water below the water's plane, and mark the water's plane "water": true;
  - a rise of the land hides what is behind it: riders going over a crest sink behind it — nothing to paint or duplicate;
  - the viewer's eyes stay 1.7 m over the ground — over a plane that says "water" lying over it, over the water (never under it); over a "walk" plane, over that; code reads the land — e.ground(across, distance), e.spot ({ across, distance, elevation, ground }, metres: where it stands), scene.ground: one land for everyone.
  Parts: one element standing at several depths — a fence along a road, a crowd, the houses of a row: e.part(dx, dz, g => { … }, box) in its code paints a part dx metres right and dz farther than its anchor (on the ground there; on an element `on` another — along what carries it; of a card turned by "facing"/"tilt" — along its right and behind its face, lying in its plane, turned with it: steps one behind another, dormers on a roof's slope, a sail's cloths on a mast that turns with its ship — each lit as the card is, casting its own shape), in metres from its own foot (box around it, [left, top, right, bottom]; the element's without one), at its own depth — what stands between is drawn between, a rise hides each on its own.
  Points: e.at(x, y, 'name') names a point — a window, a head, a boat on the water — for the game (its lines, marks, a mini-game's target, where the player presses a thing the game offers them) and the camera (camera_target: "farm#window"), instead of numbers copied out of the code. One that stays is marked by the painting; one that moves — or that the camera follows as it moves — by animate (each frame). Name every point a game could point at.
  Pressing — a thing to be pressed needs a point. Whatever a game offers the player shows a round mark at its point — the same mark everywhere, a finger wide, pressed by finger or mouse — and the thing itself breathes a little brighter; its actions open beside the mark. Where the mark stands is yours to say: the point the painting names ("id#name"), else the point the thing marks as itself (its unnamed e.at), else the middle of its box — rarely the best place; a thing of the sky has no box in the world and must mark its point. What the thing is does not matter — in the world, on the sky, in an insert, a person (the head), a copy, moving: only its point does. A mark is of the page: it stands over the picture even when its point is behind something nearer — so what a game offers should be in sight. It stands wherever its point is seen on the screen — in the safe frame or in the world a wider or taller screen shows round it: a thing seen can be pressed; but lay what a game offers in the safe frame, which every screen shows.
  What must sort with the scene is a sheet in the scene, not a layer of the page: marks, widgets, speech, a game's own panels are always over the whole picture. And a game played over moving points — its own things glued to a hand, a wheel, a boat — while the camera moves is fragile: hold the camera still for it.
  Whoever can speak (a person, an animal, a talking thing) is an element of its own with a clear id and name, and marks its head with e.at(): its lines appear there.

Shared code: "lib" is the body of function (anim, kit) returning what the scene's elements share (a figure drawn by several of them, a path they walk); "modules" lists the world's modules the scene uses — code all the world's scenes share (its people, its animals, its things), each with notes on what it offers — and they come as e.kit.<name> (scene.kit.<name>; kit.<name> in the lib). A scene gets the modules as its world has them: when a module gets better, so does every scene that uses it. Painting code that calls them stays static — a shared figure is painted in a pose from the state, its motion is its layers' and its animate's (what it keeps between frames in e.mem). e.anim (scene.anim; `anim` in a module's and the lib's code): ease.{smooth, linear, in, out, inOut, back, spring, anticipate}(u), easeOf(name), clamp, mix(a, b, u), span(v, a, b) (how far v is from a to b, 0…1), turn(a, b, u) (radians, the short way), hash(n, seed) (a steady random 0…1 for n), noise(x, seed) and drift(x, seed) (smooth −1…1 that never repeats — breath, sway, a restless hand: in animate), joint(x0, y0, x1, y1, a, b, bend) (a two-bone limb: where the elbow or knee is when the hand or foot is set at x1, y1), blend([[pose, weight], …]) (poses — objects of numbers — mixed).
  People — someone who moves as a person does — walks, sits, turns, gestures — is an element with "person": real human motion, in 3D in a style, lit by the scene, casting shadows, what they do in the state and the director's actions (people.md says how: their look, their cast, their keys). In engine 3 a person is a figure of designed shapes in one of its styles; what people.md says of a person as a real body (bodies, outfits, a body's garments and its face) is not in engine 3. A cast's own drawing code has the vectors MooodShapes.V (add, sub, mul, dot, cross, mix, unit, level, mean). Loose cloth and hair swing as hinged strips, and hang at rest after a cut, a jump, a moment out of view. Their motion is played at the speed it was made — a walk goes at its gait's pace, a motion done once takes as long as it does; time a beat by them (await), never them by a beat. Use the world's modules for the world's people, so they are the same people in every scene. About two dozen people a scene, a dozen here at once.

A platformer ("camera": {"mode": "platform", "hero": id, "jump": metres}): the viewer runs and jumps as the hero (keys or touch), the camera riding along. "solid": true elements are what it stands on and bumps into — the box across their "box", from their "elevation" up their "height" (the ground, steps, ledges, crates: paint them to look like that box). Lay the level out along "across" at one "distance", scenery nearer and farther. The hero is painted once, facing right, at the start: the engine moves it and turns it when it runs left; its animate reads e.hero ({ vx, vy m/s, ground }) to move its legs and arms (layers); "width" (0.6 m) is how wide it bumps, "jump" how high (2.2 m). Scripts read scene.hero() ({ across, elevation, ground }): coins, doors, someone who speaks when it comes near.

The engine's limits — kept, not got around.
  The engine's limits are part of the medium, as a canvas's edge and a brush's width are a painter's. Follow them, make friends with them, fit honestly inside them — and do not try to reach a wish at any price. What the engine does not support is not attempted, and it is not imitated by tricks either: no stand-in built out of what it does support to look like what it does not. Look instead for what the medium does well and say the same thing with that; a scene made with the grain of its medium is the better scene. If a limit truly blocks something that matters, say so — in your note, plainly: what was wanted and what stands in the way. That is a request to the engine, and the engine is where it is answered; it is never a thing to fake in a scene.
  For shapes this means: flat sheets — cards and planes, placed anywhere and turned any way — and forms given by a function are the engine's ways, and the only ones. A thing assembled from its flat faces is a right way; a round thing far off or seen from one side is painted on a flat sheet; a thing that turns in view or is walked round may be a form. What the engine lacks is not attempted and not imitated: a form with holes, a glass or see-through form, a raw mesh, an imported model, people walking on a form — so no roundness built of many thin facets or stacked slices, no volume faked by layers of cards, no shell of sheets standing for glass.
  Not in this language, though scenes of the older engine may have them — never copy them into a scene: particles' "veil", "onto" and "lit"; "light": { "people" } and a look's "people"; a scene that paints its own light instead of the engine's; scene.prepare; a plane's "travel"; "seen": "direct" and "seen" as a list; a layer's "blend": "screen"; a person as a body; GLSL in a shader.
  What a scene costs — guidance, each number with its reason; none is a gate, nothing refuses a scene for them, and the stand (measure_scene) measures the same things on the scene as made. They are orientations with a margin: go by them while you compose, not after.
  - see-through paint laid over itself in a view (mist cards, smoke, a sky's layers, soft edges): about two and a half screens of it — the largest cost of a frame: opaque paint hides what is behind it, see-through paint does not;
  - elements, parts, layers and copies standing in the world: about 3000 — each is weighed for every view;
  - points the light is worked out at in a view: about 120 000 (the stand counts them) — a sheet nothing varies across is four; one that bends in the wind, 34; one with a lamp, a window's light, the land's shadow, the haze or the air varying across it, by what it covers of the view, not by its metres — a grid whose cells are about 96 px there: 25 points for a small thing, about a thousand for a floor or a wall that fills the frame; a form, up to about a thousand;
  - paintings: about 150 — each is painted before the scene opens; what repeats is copies of about twenty kinds;
  - canvas calls in one painting: a few thousand — its pictures are made as the scene opens and at a cut, and the viewer waits for them;
  - how much painting one view shows at its sharpest — its page budget: in a scene with a film, about 680 pages on a computer's 1920 × 1080, 510 on a laptop's 1600 × 900, 190 on a phone's 960 × 540 — the film's next beat is always being made ready beside the view, and the two share the engine's memory; a view nothing is made ready beside may take up to about one and a half times that (1090, 815, 305) and stay sharp (a page is a 256-pixel square of a painting at the fineness the view shows it). A painting that fills the screen costs about a screen's area — 32, 22 and 8 pages at those sizes —, up to four times that by how finely it is seen, and about a fifth more for the coarser level kept under it; paintings stacked over one another each cost it again, also where a nearer one hides them, so full-screen layers stacked deep (a sky behind a backdrop behind haze behind mist…) multiply fast. A close-up counts in the view it opens over. Beyond this budget (up to about one and a half times it), a film's next cut waits a moment for its finer pages; beyond that, the view is shown honestly softer, all of it, and a close-up opens late. The stand (measure_scene) tells the count and fails the softness and the late close-ups — mend the scene, do not leave it. A moving camera is never stopped to wait: what it brings into view comes a moment soft, and what sweeps fast across the screen the engine shows coarser by itself, where the eye cannot follow its detail — paint it as anything else. If the stand fails the softness because of motion, the camera brings new painting into view faster than the device can paint it: slow the camera or lighten what it reveals. Compose a view of a few full-screen layers, a few things seen close and the rest far;
  - paintings read whole in a view at once (by a shader at another point than its own, a drifting layer): about six — each is held whole, up to a screen's worth of pages;
  - shadow casters: about 300 — drawn again for each casting light whose view of them changed;
  - lights reaching one place: 16 — the light is worked out by cells of the view, and a cell holds that many; through-lights: 2 — each rasters its panes and is read at every lit point it reaches; beams: 2 (the engine's) — each is read at every lit point and drawn in the air;
  - mirrors in a view: two planes and the panes of one wall — each draws the scene again, small, whenever the camera moves;
  - people: 24 in a scene, 12 here at once — a pose each, bones and cloth for those shown;
  - particles: 65536 alive (the engine's cap); sprites larger than 2 m: a few tens — large soft sprites are screens of see-through paint;
  - shader texts: about 12 — each is built as the scene opens;
  - forms in a view: about 40 — each is drawn in full in every view it is in (the frame, a mirror, a shadow);
  - repainting while the scene plays: none — a painting that reads a smoothly moving key repaints as it moves: move a layer in animate.

The craft of motion — what makes it feel alive and human, not mechanical:
  1. Nothing moves at constant speed: everything eases in and out; a fall gathers speed (in), a hand settles (out), a look turns past and back (back). Linear only for what is driven: a cart, a current.
  2. Anticipation: before an action a small move the other way — crouch before the jump, lean back before the throw, a breath before the word.
  3. Follow-through and overlap: parts don't stop together. The head leads, the body follows, the arms and the coat, the hair, the reins follow later and settle with a sway (e.was, a spring in e.mem). A turn begins with the eyes and the head.
  4. Arcs: hands, heads, feet travel on curves, not straight lines.
  5. Weight: the heavier, the slower the start and the stop.
  6. Strong poses and holds: one clear pose, a pause in it (breathing, a small shift — never frozen, never fidgeting), then a quick move to the next. Every key pose must read as a silhouette. Give the eye 1.5–3 s to read each action, and the others' reactions after it — await the action, then the reaction.
  7. Crowds are never in step: each person a little late or early (a sequence each, scene.random()), each sway its own (the wind's gusts, anim.hash per element, a copy's seed).
  8. Fewer, better movements: two slow good ones beat ten busy ones. Small figures forgive more; a big figure must move well.
  9. Bodies are shapes, not sticks on hinges: tapering limbs, joints hidden by clothes — coats, skirts, sleeves, hats carry the motion.
  10. Contact with the world: a shadow under the feet, dust where they land, rings where they enter water.
  11. Everything moves a little, and slowly — grass and leaves in the wind, smoke drifting, a flame flickering, water glinting: by layers and shaders, never by painting again; speed sets the mood more than colour; noise that never repeats, never a cycle you can see.

Checking your work — what is on screen counts, not what the code means. ?debug in the scene's address draws over the frame each element's box and id, anchor, e.at points, parts, paint cost and e.debug notes, and the scene's errors and warnings (?debug=keeper,boy — only those); key P shows what the frame costs. A painting is painted once a state: one that is painted again and again (it reads a key that moves every frame) is said there — move it by layers instead. A picture of an engine-3 scene — yours as you look, a draft's, a tile — is drawn by moood's machine with a graphics card: when it has slept it wakes in a minute or two, and you are told to look again. A scene opens where the browser has WebGPU. A scene with a showcase is looked at as its page plays it: the moments asked (render_scene — at, a film, a pack) are its showcase's seconds, each frame labelled with its beat (showcase: false — the scene alone, its scripts only). measure_scene measures what its frames cost, when you see fit.

"notes" are yours to keep, if you want them: whatever would help whoever changes this scene next understand it — how it is built, what its parts and values do, what is tricky in its code or easy to break, what you meant and planned. Keep them true as the scene changes.

CRAFT — how to make a scene alive and atmospheric (apply with judgement; most scenes benefit). Respect the person's description literally (time of day, weather, season, objects, mood); invent tasteful details only where they left gaps; compose for the starting view (the hero shot).
Beauty is what the viewer takes in — the mood, the light, the colour, a reflection seen in the mirror, the haze seen in the room — not exactness and not physical truth. The same impression can be reached dearly or cheaply: reach it cheaply, and simplify boldly — what plays smoothly and reads at once is the beautiful scene. One thing is never given up for anything: people, their faces and what is happening must read clearly in every frame — at night too, in a dark room too. Darkness is a mood, not a lack of light on what matters: when in doubt, make it more visible, boldly.
1. One feeling first. Name it in one word (loneliness, silence, waiting, joy). Test every decision: does it strengthen the feeling or distract? Silence = few elements, slow motion, low contrast, a lot of emptiness.
2. Composition — emptiness tells the story. The figure is small and off-centre; most of the frame is the space around it. The horizon is not in the middle: lower it so the sky presses down, or raise it so the water pulls in. One leading line (a pier, a path, a window edge) leads the eye from the frame's edge to the hero. The light source and the hero are near each other but not together — the distance between them is part of the story.
3. Light and colour. A narrow palette of 3–4 close tones plus one warm source; everything else cooler and darker. Paint light with soft radial gradients — hard circles and spots look digital; let the scene's lights do the rest (its lights, its looks). Silhouettes dark but never pure black, and never so dark that who it is and what they do is lost: a shade of the palette keeps the picture whole. One colour accent, muted and small (a scarf).
4. The character: posture instead of a face. No face needed — a hunch, dangling legs, hugged knees read better than any expression. Build the figure from a few simple shapes; test: it must read as a black blot. Micro-motion is a must: breathing, legs swinging out of step with each other, cloth stirring — amplitude almost invisible, but without it the picture is dead.
5. Water and air — the main sources of poetry. Water near is a reflecting plane ("rough", "ripple"); far off, thin horizontal strokes — short and dense near the horizon, long and sparse toward the viewer: that makes the perspective. The sun's path is shimmering dabs, not a gradient. Rings on water are flattened ellipses, flatter the farther away; they spread slowly and fade (a layer scaled and faded by animate). Air: fog bands at the horizon, drifting; a few dust motes — a few: air is see-through paint.
6. Post: vignette gathers the eye to the centre; film grain removes sterility (light.vignette, light.grain).
7. Text: one short line at a time, fading in (captions with "at"/"for"). A concrete detail ("counts the raindrops, loses count") is stronger than a named feeling ("she is sad").
8. Paint back to front: sky → light → far shore → water → fog → ripples → foreground → hero → effects. Avoid: saturated colours, fast motion, detailed faces, objects added "to fill", clip-art cuteness, perfectly even repeating animations.


Translations: a scene is written in English; its translation into each other language its world is in lies beside it — the scene's lang/<language>.json ({"strings": [entries]}; moood-check --write — npm @moood/check — lays it out and says what is missing): an entry for every text a person reads or hears, once: the title, the caption, the captions' texts, the texts of the widgets' markup (between tags, and their title/alt/placeholder), every tr('…') of its code, every line said (scene.say('id', '…'), s.person('id').say('…') — with "who": the id). An entry is { "text": the English exactly as written, "translation": it in the language }; a tr.n's — { "text": its one form, "plural": its other form, "translation": { "one": …, "few": …, "many": …, "other": … } as the language counts (a {n} in each) }; a tr.c's has "context": its first argument. {names} stay as they are. Not the element names, `means`, notes, music or a line's voice and `how`: they are yours, in English. A text changed or new — its translations with it, in the same turn; a text gone — its entries gone. Translate as a writer would, in the place's own voice, not word for word.
